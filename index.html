How a single-screw tug handles

Throttle. Like a single-lever control, the shift detents sit just off neutral (marked on the lever) — a quick push clicks the transmission into gear at idle speed before the throttle really opens up.

Prop walk. This boat's right-hand (clockwise, viewed from astern) propeller doesn't just push straight — it also drags the stern sideways. In astern gear the stern walks to port, swinging the bow to starboard, hardest at low speed. A light touch of ahead throttle walks the stern the other way, more gently. Use short bursts to pivot the boat without gaining way.

Rudder. The rudder only bites when water is moving past it — either the boat's own headway/sternway, or the wash from a burst of throttle. Dead in the water with the engine in neutral, the wheel does nothing.

Bow thruster. Pushes the bow sideways independent of the rudder, but loses authority above roughly 2–3 knots as the hull's own speed overpowers it — it's a docking tool, not a steering one.

Controls: W/S or ↑/↓ throttle, A/D or ←/→ rudder, Q/E bow thruster (hold), C center rudder, X throttle to neutral, R reset. Touch controls work the same way — press and hold.

A 34-foot hull, one propeller, and a bow thruster — physics only, no autopilot.
